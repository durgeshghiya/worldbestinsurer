# Index strategy — what we let Google index, and why

**Status: deliberate contraction, June 2026.** The indexable surface went
from ~4,450 URLs to ~1,530. This was not cleanup; it was a strategic
decision. Read this before adding pages back.

## What forced it

Two AdSense reviews were rejected for "Low value content" (April, June
2026). Between them we added roughly 250,000 words of editorial across
product, insurer and comparison pages. The second rejection was identical
to the first.

Search Console over the same period:

| | Apr 2026 | Jun 2026 (90d) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Impressions | 8,600 | 34,800 |
| Clicks | 17 | **2** |
| CTR | 0.2% | **0%** |
| Avg position | 60 | 56.8 |

Impressions quadrupled while clicks collapsed. Google was showing the
pages and nobody was choosing them. That is the definition of low value,
and no amount of additional word count was going to change it.

## The diagnosis we got wrong the first time

"Low value content" was read as "pages are too short," so the fix was to
generate more text — `ProductEditorial`, `InsurerEditorial`, `VSEditorial`
and `InsurerVSEditorial` all compose prose from structured fields and
percentile bands. The output reads well and every claim is checkable, but
it is still template-assembled text produced at scale across thousands of
near-identical pages. That is the exact shape of Google's
*scaled content abuse* policy. We made pages longer without making a
single one worth clicking.

The correct reading of "low value" is **per-page: would a human choose
this result over the other nine?** For a page that pairs two arbitrary
products, the answer is no, and multiplying it by 1,800 makes the whole
domain look worse, not better.

## What is de-indexed (`noindex, follow`)

| Route | URLs | Why |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `/vs/[slug]` | ~50 | Combinatorial product pairs. Nobody searches "Plan A vs Plan B" for arbitrary pairs. |
| `/[country]/vs/[slug]` | ~600 | Same, multiplied by country. |
| `/[country]/vs/insurer/[slug]` | ~1,166 | Pairwise insurer permutations — the single largest block of manufactured URLs. |
| `/compare/[cat]/in/[city]` | ~1,200 | Plan data is identical to the national page; only the city name in the copy differs. Tier-3 was already de-indexed; the tier-1/2 carve-out was tried and earned nothing, so all tiers now go. |
| `/[country]/compare/[cat]/in/[city]` | — | Same. |

`follow` is kept everywhere so internal link equity still flows through to
the product and insurer pages these link to. The pages remain live and
usable — they are simply not candidates for search results.

They are also removed from `sitemap.ts`. Advertising a `noindex` URL in a
sitemap is a contradictory signal and wastes crawl budget.

## Duplicate tree, collapsed

`getProductById(id)` without a country code resolves the same record as
`getProductById(id, cc)`, so **every product was published at two URLs**
with two different templates:

```
/product/hdfc-ergo-optima-secure      (855 words, older template)
/in/product/hdfc-ergo-optima-secure   (937 words, tabs + reviews + context)
```

Same for `/insurer/[slug]` vs `/[country]/insurer/[slug]`. Both self-
canonicalled, so they competed. The top-level routes now canonical to
their country-scoped twin — the richer page wins, signals consolidate, and
the duplicate drops out of the index without a `noindex` that would throw
away accumulated equity.

## What stays indexed (~1,530)

- Core and policy pages (16) — About, Methodology, Disclaimer, Privacy,
  Contact, Contact Directory, FAQ, Insurers directory, Insights, Waitlist,
  the four `/compare/[category]` hubs
- Country hubs (12) — each carries hand-written market narrative from
  `src/lib/country-narrative.ts`, genuinely different per country
- Country compare hubs (48) — the actual comparison tool, real utility
- Country product pages (1,065) — the catalog. Someone searching a plan by
  name wants exactly this, and the underlying data is specific and useful.
- Country insurer pages (248)
- Learn (135), Reports (4), Finance (growing), author pages

Every one of these now carries an explicit self-canonical. That was not
true before: removing the layout-level canonical fallback in `bf36e61`
fixed the dynamic routes but left 17 static pages with no canonical at
all. Fixed in the same commit as this document.

## Rules going forward

1. **Do not add page types that are permutations of existing data.** If
   the page is generated by crossing two lists, it does not get indexed.
2. **New indexable pages must answer a question someone actually types.**
   The test is not word count; it is whether a human would pick this
   result.
3. **Do not resubmit to AdSense yet.** Give Google 6–8 weeks to drop the
   de-indexed URLs and re-assess the domain, and only resubmit once
   Search Console shows clicks — not impressions — moving.
4. **Products are on probation.** 1,065 templated pages is still a lot
   relative to the editorial base. If clicks have not moved in ~8 weeks,
   the next cut is to a curated subset (flagship plans, high-confidence
   records, primary markets) rather than the whole catalog.

## Expected behaviour after deploy

Search Console will report a large rise in "Excluded by 'noindex' tag" and
indexed pages will fall from ~9,000 toward ~1,500 over several weeks.
**That is the intended outcome, not a regression.** Impressions will drop
too. The metric that matters is clicks and average position on the pages
that remain.
